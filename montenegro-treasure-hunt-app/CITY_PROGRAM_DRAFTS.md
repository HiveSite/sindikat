# Montenegro Treasure Hunt - City Program Drafts

> STATUS: CONTENT DESIGN DRAFTS. NONE OF THESE ROUTES SHOULD GO LIVE BEFORE ON-SITE WALKING, GPS TESTING AND SAFETY VALIDATION.

The platform engine is reusable. The city content is not. Each city needs its own physical evidence, narrative identity and final location.

Use these drafts as the starting backlog inside Program Builder.

# 1. CETINJE - THE ARCHIVE OF TWO MONTENEGROS

## Product fit

Recommended as the first expansion after Podgorica.

Why:
- compact historic core
- unusually dense concentration of state, royal, cultural and religious landmarks
- excellent setting for an archive / conflicting-records story
- can be designed largely around exterior observation rather than paid museum entry

Reference context: National Tourism Organisation highlights King Nikola's Palace, Biljarda, Cetinje Monastery and Cetinje's museum concentration.

## Target format

- 6-8 stops
- 75-110 minutes
- medium difficulty
- target 8-16 teams
- 1-2 teams per starting stop preferred

## Story concept

**Working title: The State That Existed Twice**

A researcher discovers two apparently official versions of the same historical handover. Both look authentic. Each field stop unlocks one layer of the record: royal, civic, religious, diplomatic, personal and archival. The team must determine why two records describe the same decision differently.

Central question:

> How can two official records both be authentic and still tell different versions of the same moment?

The mystery should be fictional. Real history provides atmosphere and observable field evidence, not invented claims presented as historical fact.

## Candidate route

### CT01 - Dvorski trg / Palace approach
Purpose: establish power, ceremony and official image.

Field-task prototype:
- identify one architectural feature that clearly marks the building as ceremonial rather than residential
- FIELD TEST must choose one permanent, unambiguous exterior answer

Story beat:
- a formal public record establishes Version A

### CT02 - King Nikola's Palace exterior
Purpose: personal record versus state record.

Field-task prototype:
- compare symmetry / entrance hierarchy / façade detail
- answer must be visible without entering the museum

Story beat:
- a private note contradicts the tone, not the facts, of Version A

### CT03 - Biljarda exterior
Purpose: writing, planning and authorship.

Field-task prototype:
- identify a fixed exterior detail that distinguishes the complex from the previous palace stop
- do not use interior billiard-room access as a requirement

Story beat:
- a draft reveals that the document had an earlier wording

### CT04 - Cetinje Monastery approach / public exterior zone
Purpose: another institution preserving memory.

Field-task prototype:
- use spatial relationship: gate / courtyard / building orientation or another permanent public detail
- do not require entering worship areas or interrupting religious activity

Story beat:
- a third source confirms the event happened, but not how it was described

### CT05 - Vladin dom / National Museum exterior candidate
Purpose: institutional archive.

Field-task prototype:
- use a permanent façade inscription, emblem or architectural element after field verification

Story beat:
- an archival catalogue number reveals two copies were filed under different categories

### CT06 - Vlaška Church exterior candidate
Purpose: time depth and physical continuity.

Field-task prototype:
- select a respectful exterior architectural observation
- avoid tasks involving sacred objects or requiring entry

Story beat:
- an older witness record shows the wording difference started before the final filing

### CT07 - Blue Palace / diplomatic quarter exterior candidate
Purpose: external audience versus internal record.

Field-task prototype:
- compare formal frontage with the earlier institutional buildings

Story beat:
- a diplomatic copy proves one version was written for an external audience

### CT08 - Final central public space to field-test
Purpose: resolution in a neutral civic place.

Final deduction:
- identify which record was internal
- identify which was public-facing
- identify the editing event that created the apparent contradiction

Final meaning:
- two texts can describe one event differently because they were written for different audiences, without either being a forgery

## Field validation priorities

- museum opening hours must never be required for the base route
- religious sites require respectful public-space tasks only
- confirm all points can be reached without crossing unsafe traffic patterns
- validate GPS around larger institutional buildings

# 2. KOTOR - THE CAPTAIN'S MISSING LEDGER

## Product fit

Premium flagship candidate.

The old town is compact and highly legible, with gates, squares, churches, palaces and maritime identity. The official Kotor tourism site presents the Old Town and walls as central attractions, while Montenegro tourism lists the Clock Tower, Cathedral of St Tryphon, St Luke and other major heritage landmarks.

## Target format

- 8 stops
- 75-105 minutes
- medium-hard
- target 8-16 teams
- avoid fortress climb in the standard route

## Story concept

**Working title: The Captain's Missing Ledger**

A ship returned to Kotor with the right cargo but the wrong ledger. Eight surviving records - gate entry, time, cargo mark, family note, church record, maritime record, route note and final receipt - appear to implicate different people.

Central question:

> If the ship, cargo and crew returned, what exactly went missing?

Resolution direction:
- not stolen cargo
- a ledger page was intentionally separated to protect a person, transaction or identity
- final deduction reconstructs who had which version and why

## Candidate route

### KO01 - Sea Gate
Physical evidence: gateway / threshold / inscription or fixed emblem to verify on site.

Task prototype:
- identify which permanent feature marks this as a formal entrance rather than an ordinary street opening

Story beat:
- port-entry record confirms arrival

### KO02 - Square of Arms / Clock Tower
Physical evidence: public timekeeper and square geometry.

Task prototype:
- identify the feature that communicates public time

Story beat:
- ledger and witness agree on hour

### KO03 - St Luke's Square
Physical evidence: small-square spatial relationship and church façades.

Task prototype:
- compare two permanent façade/plan details visible from the square

Story beat:
- a witness statement introduces the first contradiction

### KO04 - Cathedral of St Tryphon exterior
Physical evidence: paired towers / façade composition - exact task to be field-tested.

Task prototype:
- choose a stable exterior count or symmetry observation

Story beat:
- a formal record confirms a name but not the transaction

### KO05 - Maritime Museum / Grgurina Palace exterior
Physical evidence: palace façade / maritime institutional context.

Task prototype:
- use a permanent exterior architectural marker; do not require museum admission

Story beat:
- ship inventory reveals one numbered page is missing

### KO06 - River Gate / Northern Gate
Physical evidence: relationship between gate, wall and water.

Task prototype:
- identify which side of the defensive system the gate addresses using visible geography

Story beat:
- route note shows the missing page left the main commercial path

### KO07 - Gurdic Gate
Physical evidence: southern defensive entrance / moat relationship.

Task prototype:
- verify a structural difference between this gate and Sea Gate

Story beat:
- a second exit record changes the suspected sequence

### KO08 - Walls / Kampana public exterior zone candidate
Physical evidence: wall-water-city relationship.

Task prototype:
- identify the three layers around the team: old town / fortification / water

Final clue:
- bring the records to the point where trade, defense and water are visible together

Final deduction:
- which ledger page was removed
- who benefited from the removal
- which two records prove the cargo itself was never missing

## Operational cautions

- GPS drift can be significant between high stone buildings; use larger radii only where the physical observation prevents false completion
- cruise-season congestion requires live testing at peak hours
- standard route should remain at street level; do not require St John fortress stairs
- paid museum/cathedral entry should be optional bonus content only

# 3. BUDVA - THE CITY WITH TOO MANY NAMES

## Product fit

Strong compact seasonal route. Tourism Organisation of Budva documents the Old Town's gates, squares, walls, Citadel, churches, archaeological remains and bilingual landmark signage.

## Target format

- 8 stops
- 60-90 minutes
- medium difficulty
- ideal 8-16 teams
- morning / shoulder-season operation preferred in peak summer

## Story concept

**Working title: The City With Too Many Names**

A fragment of an old city register contains different names for the same place across different eras. Teams recover layers from the gate, walls, church quarter, archaeological traces and civic spaces.

Central question:

> When a city changes rulers, language and map labels, which part of its identity actually stays the same?

Final answer should be about continuity of place, not a disputed historical claim.

## Candidate route

### BU01 - Velja vrata / principal Old Town entrance candidate
Task prototype:
- identify a permanent structural feature proving the team is at a fortified entrance

Story beat:
- oldest surviving map label

### BU02 - Trg pjesnika
Task prototype:
- use fixed signage / spatial geometry rather than temporary event furniture

Story beat:
- a newer civic name appears

### BU03 - Citadel exterior
Task prototype:
- identify one visible defensive element
- interior access not required

Story beat:
- military record uses another version of the city name

### BU04 - Church quarter / St John exterior
Official tourism notes the tall St John tower as a dominant feature.

Task prototype:
- identify which vertical element dominates the church cluster

Story beat:
- religious record preserves the older name

### BU05 - Santa Maria in Punta exterior
Task prototype:
- determine how the building relates physically to the wall / sea edge

Story beat:
- a dated fragment narrows the era of one record

### BU06 - Pizanela / Pizane gate candidate
Task prototype:
- compare this smaller gate with the main entry

Story beat:
- merchant note uses a practical local nickname rather than official label

### BU07 - Old Town walls / Brijeg tower exterior candidate
Task prototype:
- identify a defensive relationship visible from ground level

Story beat:
- reconstruction shows the city changed labels while the wall line stayed recognizable

### BU08 - Archaeological marker / ancient-gate or pilon signage candidate
Budva Tourism Organisation has marked archaeological and historic structures around the Old Town.

Task prototype:
- use official fixed interpretive signage plus visible remains

Final deduction:
- put four names / eras in order
- identify the one physical boundary repeated in every version

## Operational cautions

- peak-summer crowding is the main risk
- tasks must survive restaurant tables, events and temporary installations
- do not rely on Citadel / museum opening hours for standard progression

# 4. NIKŠIĆ - THE CITY REBUILT FROM CLUES

## Product fit

Best candidate for a local, less tourist-dependent program. The Municipality of Nikšić lists Trg Slobode, the King Nikola monument, Cathedral, King Nikola Palace, Bedem, Trebjesa and other city landmarks.

## Target format

Start with a **5-stop compact beta**, then add stops only after a walking survey proves the central route is coherent.

- 5-7 stops
- 60-90 minutes
- easy-medium
- 6-15 teams

## Story concept

**Working title: The City Rebuilt From Clues**

A planner's folder contains five incompatible sketches of Nikšić. Each sketch preserves one feature from a different phase of the city's identity. Teams must determine which elements belong to the same urban plan and which came from earlier layers.

Central question:

> If a city is rebuilt again and again, which clues tell you what belongs to the same plan?

## Core candidate route

### NK01 - Trg Slobode
Task prototype:
- use square geometry / axes / permanent monument relationship

Story beat:
- first planning sketch establishes a central node

### NK02 - Monument to King Nikola I
Task prototype:
- identify orientation / relationship to the square after field test

Story beat:
- a dated annotation links the square to a political period

### NK03 - Cathedral / Saborna crkva exterior
Task prototype:
- use a respectful architectural observation from public space

Story beat:
- skyline sketch establishes one visual anchor

### NK04 - King Nikola Palace exterior
Task prototype:
- compare façade role with the cathedral / square

Story beat:
- administrative drawing proves two sketches belong to the same rebuilding phase

### NK05 - Bedem
Task prototype:
- identify older defensive fabric versus later planned-city fabric

Story beat:
- the oldest layer resolves what was inherited rather than newly planned

## Optional extension - only after walking survey

- Spomenik palim borcima / Trebjesa
- other municipal landmarks that can be connected through a safe pedestrian route

Do not add Carev most or distant attractions to the core city game simply because they are famous. A coherent walk is more important than landmark count.

Final deduction:
- sort the five sketches into inherited / planned / later-added layers

# 5. STARI BAR - THE CITY THAT MOVED

## Product fit

Exceptional physical storytelling environment. National tourism sources describe Old Bar's large ruin complex, aqueduct, baths, clock tower, religious structures and fortified fabric.

Recommended product type:

**ticketed / controlled-access premium program**, not the default free public city hunt, unless all selected points are confirmed accessible from public zones.

## Target format

- 7-9 stops inside one controlled historic area
- 75-120 minutes
- medium-hard
- 6-12 teams

## Story concept

**Working title: The City That Moved**

An abandoned-city survey contains one impossible statement: a system is described as functioning after the city was supposedly already gone. Teams examine defense, water, time, domestic life and religious layers to reconstruct what disappeared, what survived and what returned.

Central question:

> When people leave a city, does the city end at once - or do its systems disappear at different times?

## Candidate route modules

Order must be established during official field walk inside the site.

### BR01 - Main entrance / fortified threshold
Task: identify a permanent defensive entrance feature.
Story: date of official abandonment claim.

### BR02 - Citadel / high defensive zone
Task: classify the function suggested by position and walls.
Story: military record continues after the claimed date.

### BR03 - Aqueduct
Task: identify the infrastructure's purpose from visible structure.
Story: water-system maintenance record introduces the contradiction.

### BR04 - Clock tower
Task: identify the feature that made public time visible.
Story: civic time record proves organized activity persisted.

### BR05 - Turkish bath remains
Task: use room / water / masonry relationship after site validation.
Story: domestic-service record adds a civilian layer.

### BR06 - Religious-building remains candidate
Task: respectful exterior identification of a fixed architectural feature.
Story: community record narrows who remained.

### BR07 - Residential / villa remains candidate
Task: distinguish domestic from defensive function using visible layout.
Story: personal inventory changes the meaning of "abandoned".

### BR08 - Final overlook / synthesis point
Final deduction:
- identify which city system lasted longest
- distinguish political abandonment from physical disappearance

## Operational cautions

- confirm ticket price, group rules and opening times for every event date
- obtain permission if commercial/group activity requires it
- do not use unstable ruins or off-path areas
- GPS may be less important inside a controlled site; physical checks can carry more verification weight

# 6. HERCEG NOVI - THE MESSAGE THAT CHANGED LEVELS

## Product fit

Very strong narrative city because of its fortresses, old town, stairs and vertical relationship between upper city and Škver. Official Herceg Novi tourism describes the old town, Kanli Kula, Forte Mare and Škver, and explicitly highlights the promenade and stairways connecting the city to the harbour.

## Target format

- 6-8 stops
- 75-110 minutes
- medium difficulty physically
- 6-12 teams initially

## Story concept

**Working title: The Message That Changed Levels**

One message appears in three versions: upper-city copy, old-town copy and harbour copy. Each is timestamped correctly, but the sequence looks impossible until teams understand how the city changes elevation and how long it takes to move between levels.

Central question:

> Can three correct timestamps form the wrong story if you ignore the city between them?

## Candidate route

### HN01 - Old Town main square / clock-tower zone candidate
Task prototype:
- identify the fixed feature that acts as a transition between levels / streets

Story beat:
- first timestamp

### HN02 - Belavista / second main square candidate
Task prototype:
- use permanent square / church relationship

Story beat:
- witness copy appears to contradict the first

### HN03 - Kanli Kula exterior/public approach
Task prototype:
- classify the structure by visible defensive features
- do not require paid entry

Story beat:
- upper-level messenger record

### HN04 - Stair-axis checkpoint
The city itself is the evidence.

Task prototype:
- verify a change of elevation / count a small fixed stair segment only after safety test
- never make speed part of the challenge

Story beat:
- travel-time note exposes the missing variable

### HN05 - Forte Mare exterior/public approach
Task prototype:
- identify physical relationship between fortress and sea

Story beat:
- lower-city copy establishes arrival near the coast

### HN06 - Škver
Official tourism describes Škver as an informal city centre and notes the promenade and stairways leading to it.

Task prototype:
- identify one permanent remnant / landmark tied to the harbour area after field verification

Story beat:
- final timestamp closes the movement sequence

### HN07 - Optional final promenade point
Use only if it improves the story and keeps the route pedestrian.

Final deduction:
- arrange the three timestamped messages
- add the missing movement/elevation segment
- identify why the original interpretation was wrong

## Operational cautions

- vertical load matters: describe difficulty honestly
- stairs become slippery in rain; define wet-weather fallback
- Forte Mare / Kanli Kula interior access should be optional unless ticketing is built into the event
- Španjola is not recommended for the standard route because official tourism itself describes it as less accessible

# 7. STANDARD TASK LIBRARY

Use these patterns when building any city. The answer must be field-tested.

## A. Landmark signature
"Which feature proves this is the intended structure?"

Best for:
- clock tower
- gate
- church exterior
- bridge
- fortress

## B. Fixed count
"How many ___ are visible on the marked façade / section?"

Rules:
- count no more than 3-8 items
- never count movable furniture, cars, flags or temporary signs

## C. Spatial relationship
"Where is X relative to Y?"

Best for:
- wall and water
- square and gate
- fortress and harbour
- park and river

## D. Compare two features
"Which of these two elements is older-looking / taller / repeated / symmetrical?"

Only use objective visual properties, not subjective beauty.

## E. Read one permanent inscription
Use a short number, year or word that can be seen legally from public space.

Never require:
- climbing
- touching protected objects
- entering restricted areas
- obstructing worship or business operations

## F. Simple transformation
Take a visible number/word and apply one clear operation:
- first letter
- last digit
- order three visible numbers
- Caesar shift provided in the task

Do not make players search the web for trivia while standing at the stop.

# 8. ROUTE CAPACITY RULES

For S stops and T teams, teams are assigned by:

`startIndex = (teamNo - 1) mod S`

Recommended density:
- 1 team/start - excellent
- 2 teams/start - excellent for larger events
- 3 teams/start - acceptable
- 4+ teams/start - redesign or stagger starts

Examples:

8 stops / 16 teams:
- 2 teams per start

8 stops / 20 teams:
- four starts receive 3 teams and four starts receive 2

10 stops / 30 teams:
- 3 teams per start

The Program Builder Launch QA already calculates this automatically.

Before marking a route Ready, record:

- Phone B accuracy

- approach direction 2
- false-trigger risk

- night suitability
- rain suitability
- opening-hours dependency
- paid-access dependency
- final field clue
- hint 1
- hint 2
- observable task
- correct answer

A stop is not "done" because its copy is good. It is done only when the real location behaves fairly on a phone.
